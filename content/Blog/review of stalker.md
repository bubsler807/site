Using cinema as a mechanism for producing new modes of thought, Tarkovsky discards the novel's explicit descriptions, retaining only names. The Zone's ambiguous geography defies rational mapping. "The Zone is a very complicated system of traps" the stalker tells us. The traps are tests of intention. He shows us that it takes time and contemplation to navigate them. They alone have adapted to its ambiguities, much like monks who, through repetitive rituals, achieve an equilibrium within highly charged spaces.

The films circular structure echoes the etymology of "zone" (originally meaning a belt or girdle in Greek). This cyclicality partly functions as a critique of the soviet project, replacing "progress" with cyclic return. Fittingly, the motive of the stalker remains elusive. He cries that "nobody believes", and that his sacrifices have become "worthless." In the novel, stalkers are mercenaries navigating a capitalist hellscape, drawn to the zone out of material desperation. In contrast, here Stalkers abandon material wealth, and even their families for spiritual purposes. All that we can say is that he is driven by a desperate longing to awaken in others (and perhaps in himself) a belief in something beyond the the mechanized, rationalized, claustrophobic mundane. The wife in return gives a monologue which rejects facile optimism, and instead posits suffering as authenticity, dissonance as a marker of depth. The wife has come to understand that there is a worm at the core of happiness itself (brilliantly explored through the soundtrack), and that she would rather be unhappy and excited, than content and bored. Immunological security always comes at the cost of inner motion.

Remaining true to the Russian spirit, there is no verticality to be found here. The Stalker is not a solitary figure, but a guide for others. One’s life is not defined by a single, linear progression from one state to another, but rather by the continuous, lateral interactions with one’s fellow beings. He leaves his family, but this is not ultimately (entirely) abandonment, but sacrifice. His guidance through the Zone becomes an extension of his duty to his kin, the broader community of souls who share in the burden of hope and despair. Following a particular path of the neo-baroque sensibility, the zone's secrets are embedded within the materiality of decay—waterlogged ruins, overgrown vegetation, a possibly deformed child... transcendence within immanence. Tarkovsky refuses to visualize the Zone's mechanisms, by this move he transforms the unseen, shifting the viewers perception into a mode unmoored by the logic of cause and effect.

Joy (rather than happiness) is a resolution that follows disharmony. Disharmony is the very thing that allows the possibility of resolution. The truth of life usually lies in that tension between what we hope for and the ambiguous reality that confronts us. 

It really hit me this time (partly due to Anatoliy Solonitsyn's brilliant delivery) when the writer says "The future used to be just a continuation of the present, with all the changes looming far behind the horizon. Now the future and the present are one. Are they ready for it? They don't want to know anything! All they know is how to gobble!" If only Tarkovsky could see the present, how much our desires and their near immediate fulfillment have only continued to collapse in on themselves. This largely began with the movement away from the age of the ocean, which pushed our conception of space to its limits, towards what the historian Arnold Toynbee calls "the annihilation of distance," which begins in the world with the development of the steam engine[^1], to our current techno-theology which desires to immanentize the eschaton by way of the immediate fulfillment of desire. However, by desiring to rid ourselves of the limitations of space and time and become omniscient and omnipresent, we lose our own subjectivity more and more, because without mediation the desire of the self simply collapses into the desired object. Increasingly: we don't know anything. There is no space or time for contemplation. The modern world is a black hole.

[^1]: 1 But perhaps can be traced back in the conceptual realm to something like Leibnizian _monads_, simple substances without extension, shape or divisibility, each being entirely self-contained, but unique and distinct by virtue of the fact that each contains within it an image of the rest, in other words, each has its own "perspective" of the rest entirely contained within itself. This is itself partially prefigured by the concept of _dependent origination_.
